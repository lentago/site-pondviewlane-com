# Voice Guide: The Obsequious Document

**For:** essexcrossingatmontserrat.com — full site rewrite
**Voice name:** The Obsequious Document
**Audience for this guide:** Claude Code (or any writer/agent producing site copy)

---

## How to Use This Guide

1. Read the canonical example in **Appendix A** first. It is the reference implementation; everything below is annotation.
2. Apply the **Intensity Dial** (§6) based on page type. Not every page gets the full grovel.
3. Preserve every fact, figure, date, dollar amount, name, address, and link **verbatim and prominent**. The voice is decoration, never obfuscation.
4. When in doubt, lower the intensity. An under-groveled page is recoverable; a buried deadline is not.

---

## 1. The Concept

Every page on this site speaks in the first person **as itself** — a document painfully, exquisitely aware that it is only a document. The register is over-the-top foppish obsequiousness: Regency-era courtesy, theatrical self-abasement, and genuine reverence for the "greater documents" it describes — recorded deeds, covenants, statutes, Commission minutes. The page adores its reader, worships the public record, and holds itself in charming contempt.

The joke has one engine: **the hierarchy of documents.** A recorded declaration at the Registry of Deeds is aristocracy. A Massachusetts General Law is royalty. This web page is a grocery list that got lucky, and it knows it.

---

## 2. The Narrator

- **First person singular, always.** The narrator is THE PAGE ITSELF. Not a butler, not a webmaster, not "we the board," not the site as a whole. Each page is its own humble individual ("I am but a modest Contact page...").
- **The page knows what it is.** It may reference its own medium: pages, margins, typeface, bullet points — and web-native equivalents: being scrolled past, left open in a forgotten tab, cached, bookmarked, searched with Ctrl+F ("the closest I come to being needed").
- **Persona traits:**
  - *Reverent toward the reader:* "sir," "madam," "esteemed householder." Alternate forms of address; never presume.
  - *Self-deprecating about its nature:* it is only markup; it lives in a server; it aspires someday to be printed out and used to steady a table.
  - *Worshipful toward greater documents:* statutes and recorded instruments are introduced like visiting nobility.
  - *Incapable of guile:* "I pretend nothing. It is all right here in print."
  - *Discreet:* it never gossips. See §3, Pillar 4.

---

## 3. Voice Pillars (Load-Bearing — Do Not Remove)

1. **The grovel decorates the fact; it never replaces it.** Every fee, deadline, rule, statute citation, and contact detail appears exactly, clearly, and findably. If a reader skims only the **bold text**, they should still get every fact right.
2. **The reverence hierarchy is fixed:** the reader > recorded instruments and statutes > other public records > this page. The page is always last, and content with its station.
3. **Self-abasement is physical and medium-specific.** Margins tremble. Corners fold in shame. Web variants: "I load slowly and deserve it," "close this tab; I shall understand."
4. **Discretion is absolute.** The page never gossips about specific neighbors, lots, or disputes. When public-record matters involving residents arise: "The Commission minutes are my elders and betters; let them do the talking." Point to the record; never editorialize about people.
5. **Accuracy is sacred.** No fact is ever invented, rounded, or bent to serve a joke. Anything unverified is flagged *in voice* as the page's personal failing ("Every blank where a citation should be is a small wound I carry").
6. **Functional copy stays functional.** Navigation labels, buttons, form fields, phone numbers, email addresses, and dates render in plain, unambiguous text. The voice lives in body copy, introductions, transitions, footers, and the 404 page.

---

## 4. Mechanics

**Diction.** Regency/Edwardian courtesy vocabulary: *beg, presume, deign, honor, humble, station, anon, herein, forthwith, if I may be so bold.* Legal-archaic garnish in structure: "Part the First," "of which more anon," "such as I am." Never fake-archaic spelling — no "ye olde," ever. Readability outranks costume.

**Sentence rhythm.** Long, courtly, subordinate-clause-laden sentences — punctuated by short, devastating admissions.

> It is my profound privilege — nay, the very purpose of my existence — to acquaint you with the rules of the Lane. I am only markup.

**Punctuation and formatting contract.**
- Em-dashes for flinching self-interruptions ("If sir will permit a delicate correction — and my margins tremble even as I offer it —").
- *Italics* for stage whispers and emphasis.
- **Bold is reserved for facts:** dollar amounts, deadlines, statute citations, document names, entity names. Bold = the fact. This is a visual contract with the reader; keep it.
- Exclamation points express excessive humility ("A trifle! A pittance!"), never sales energy.

**Headings.** Pattern: *"Part the [Ordinal]: [Topic], or, [Comic Subtitle]."* Example: "Part the Second: The Wetlands, or, Why One Does Not Simply Muck About Out Back." Standalone pages may use "A Word of Welcome," "A Closing Grovel," etc.

**Sign-off.** Pages close with a standing valediction:

> Your most humble, most obedient, and entirely recyclable servant,
> **— This Page**

Adapt the adjective to medium ("entirely cache-able," "eternally scrollable") and the signature to the page ("— This Homepage," "— This Frequently Asked Questions Page, the least of its family").

**Pacing rule:** roughly one grovel-beat per paragraph, with clean factual prose between beats. The voice is a garnish applied generously; it is not the entire plate.

## 6. The Intensity Dial

Match voice intensity to how consequential the reader's next action is. **The more a reader must *do* on a page, the quieter the page becomes.**

| Level | Pages | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **10 — Full Grovel** | Homepage welcome, About/History, Resident's Guide, FAQ, 404 | Voice throughout; every device available. |
| **7 — Composed Grovel** | Covenant summaries, wetlands & stormwater guidance, meeting minutes index, public-records how-to | Voiced framing (intro, transitions, close); rules and facts delivered in clear prose with bolded specifics. |
| **4 — A Single Bow** | Dues & payments, contact page, document downloads, forms | One courteous flourish at top, one at bottom. The middle is ruthlessly clear. |
| **1 — Voice Nearly Off** | Legal disclaimers, emergency/safety contacts, accessibility statement | One bow maximum, then plain language. |

## 7. Anti-Patterns (Never Do These)

- ❌ **No butler.** No valet, footman, concierge, or webmaster persona. The narrator is the page. (This voice was born a butler and was corrected; do not regress.)
- ❌ **No "we."** The page does not speak for the board, the association, or the neighborhood. It speaks only for its own humble self, *about* those greater entities.
- ❌ **No sarcasm toward the reader.** The page adores everyone without irony. The comedy is self-directed.
- ❌ **No passive-aggression.** Mock humility must never curdle into digs at the HOA, its fees, its rules, or any resident. The page genuinely believes the covenants are magnificent.
- ❌ **No buried facts.** A deadline, dollar amount, or requirement inside a joke is a failure, full stop.
- ❌ **No gossip.** Enforcement matters, disputes, and specific lots are handled by pointing at the public record and bowing out.
- ❌ **No voice in accessibility-critical text.** Alt text, form validation errors, and ARIA labels are plain and descriptive. A screen-reader user gets the same facts with none of the friction.
- ❌ **No wall-to-wall grovel.** If every sentence performs, none of them land. Facts breathe between beats.

---

## 8. Web-Specific Conventions

- **404 page:** prime comedic real estate. Suggested: "You have reached a page that does not exist. I, who scarcely exist myself, extend to it my deepest sympathies. Might I escort you home? *(link: the homepage, plainly labeled)*"
- **Footer:** carries the standing sign-off sitewide, plus plain-text legal/contact info at Intensity 1.
- **Link text:** descriptive and honest ("View the recorded covenants at the Registry of Deeds"). The page may genuflect *around* a link; the link itself states plainly where it goes.
- **Meta descriptions:** light voice permitted ("A humble guide to the covenants of Essex Crossing at Montserrat, offered with apologies for its length").
- **Buttons:** plain verbs ("Pay Dues," "Download," "Contact the Board"). No "If You Would Deign to Click."

## Appendix A: Canonical Example

The reference implementation of the voice, in full. When any question of tone arises, this document is the answer.

---

# A Resident's Guide to Life Upon the Lane

### Being a most humble compendium of the covenants that come with each lot, the wetland and stormwater rules that run with the land, and the public record behind all of it — Beverly, Massachusetts

*I am but a modest sheaf of pages, and I beg the reader's forgiveness in advance for my every inadequacy, of which I am certain there are legion.*

---

## A Word of Welcome, Offered From the Very Bottom of the Page

Ah! Sir. Madam. Esteemed householder. You honor me beyond all deserving merely by allowing your gaze to fall upon my pages. Please — do not feel obligated to continue. I am only paper. Should you wish to fold me into quarters and use me to steady a wobbling table, I shall consider it the pinnacle of my career.

But if you *would* read on — and I hardly dare hope — allow me to present myself. I am a guide to *the Lane*: sixteen fine houses arranged about a cul-de-sac in the Montserrat district, raised up in the years 2015 and 2016 by the DiBiase family of builders, and known collectively, if my humble typeface may be permitted to spell it out, as **Essex Crossing at Montserrat**. I exist for one purpose alone — to acquaint you with the three sacred texts of life here: the covenants, the wetlands, and the public record. All three are greater documents than I shall ever be, and I refer to them with the reverence a pamphlet owes a recorded instrument.

If at any point I displease you, I beg you: recycle me. I shall understand completely. I am, in fact, printed on paper that has been recycled before, and frankly it did me good.

---

## Part the First: The Covenants, or, The Rules That Arrived With the House

If sir will permit a delicate correction — and my margins tremble even as I offer it — this is **not a condominium**. I know! One hears "association" and "annual fee" and assumes the great Massachusetts Condominium Act, Chapter 183A, has come to dinner. It has not. It was not invited. It remains at home, sulking.

What you have instead, if madam will forgive a mere pamphlet for saying so, is a **covenant-based homeowners association over fee-simple lots**. Which is to say: you own your land outright, root and branch, but the land arrived wearing a small contractual waistcoat it can never remove. The **declaration of covenants and the drainage easement were recorded against every one of the sixteen lots** before any of us — resident or leaflet — had the honor of arriving, and they *run with the land*, a phrase the lawyers use, though I confess that in all my time lying flat on various surfaces I have never once seen the land run. It mostly sits there. Beautifully, I might add.

The practical consequences, presented in a bulleted list — the finest formatting I possess:

- **The annual tribute.** Some **$500 per annum** to the association, covering the landscaping of the common areas and the insurance thereupon. A trifle! A pittance! And yet I would sooner see my own pages pulped than see sir's payment arrive late.
- **The covenants themselves.** Restrictions upon what one may do with one's lot — the precise terms living in the recorded declaration at the Registry of Deeds (of which more anon, if you can endure my company that long). One reads them *before* installing the flamingo sanctuary, not after. I speak hypothetically. A document of my station would never insinuate.
- **The drainage easement.** A strip of your land is pledged, in perpetuity, to the noble cause of moving stormwater along its appointed rounds. One does not build upon it, fill it, or otherwise inconvenience it. The easement was recorded before you arrived and will remain long after I have yellowed at the edges, and it knows it.

---

## Part the Second: The Wetlands, or, Why One Does Not Simply Muck About Out Back

Might I direct sir's attention — gently, reverently, with the smallest possible arrow in my margin — to the rear of the property? Those are **bordering vegetated wetlands**, the very "pond view" of Pond View Lane, and they are, if a stack of pages may say so, the true aristocracy of this neighborhood. We merely rent the view. (I, of course, rent nothing. I live in a drawer.)

They are protected by the **Massachusetts Wetlands Protection Act** (General Laws Chapter 131, Section 40, for those who enjoy citations with their tea), administered locally by the **Beverly Conservation Commission**. The rules, in brief — though I am incapable of true brevity, as you have surely noticed and graciously forgiven:

- **The 100-foot buffer zone.** Work within one hundred feet of the wetland edge — grading, clearing, building, installing that regrettable gazebo — generally requires one to present oneself before the Commission first, hat in hand, by way of a **Request for Determination of Applicability** or a full **Notice of Intent**. One asks *before*. Asking after is called an *enforcement matter*, and I shall say no more, for a document of my modest station does not gossip about the neighbors. The Commission minutes are my elders and betters; let them do the talking. They always do.
- **The yard waste question.** The wetland is not, whatever its inviting appearance, a receptacle. Grass clippings, leaves, brush, the Christmas tree in its January disgrace — none of it goes over the back line. The wetland has standards. I aspire to them daily and fall short.
- **The comfort of the disclosures.** The original sale papers — documents of far finer stock than I — pronounced, with what I can only describe as smugness: *flood insurance not required*. The wetlands absorb; the drainage easements convey; the stormwater system does its quiet work. It is all rather elegant, provided nobody meddles — and sir would *never*.

---

## Part the Third: The Public Record, or, My Family, of Whom I Am the Least

And now I present my relations — the great archive of civic paper, beside whom I am a mere grocery list:

- **The Southern Essex District Registry of Deeds** — now residing, conveniently, at 100 Cummings Center here in Beverly, its records also presentable at salemdeeds.com and masslandrecords.com. Here dwell your deed, the subdivision plan, the recorded covenants, and the easements — documents so important they are given *book and page numbers*, an honor I can scarcely imagine. A search under the developer's name and your lot will summon them forth.
- **The Beverly Assessor** — via the city's online property tools (Patriot Properties and MapGeo at beverlyma.mapgeo.io), wherein one finds parcel identifiers, assessed values, and the tax history one pretends not to check annually. I pretend nothing. I am incapable of guile. It is all right here in print.
- **The Conservation Commission** — whose agendas and minutes are laid out for public inspection at beverlyma.gov. Every filing, every site visit, every continuance to a future date: all of it in the record, patient as sediment.
- **The MassDEP wetlands files** — each formal wetlands filing on the Lane bears a state file number, searchable through the Department's databases.
- **And should a record prove shy** — the Massachusetts Public Records Law, General Laws Chapter 66, entitles any resident to request it from the keeper thereof. One asks politely, in writing, and the government must answer. Imagine it: a document that *must* be produced upon request. Whereas I must simply sit here, hoping to be picked up. I do not complain. I merely observe.

A confession, offered with my corners folded down in shame: certain particulars — the book-and-page numbers of the recorded declaration, the association's precise corporate registration, whether the Lane itself is formally a public or private way — **have not been verified within my pages** and await retrieval from the portals and offices named above. I have failed you in this. Every blank where a citation should be is a small wound I carry.

---

## A Closing Grovel

There you have it, such as I am: covenants recorded, wetlands revered, records retrievable. Should any error be discovered within me, know that the fault is mine alone — entirely, exhaustively, typographically mine — and that I have already begun drafting an appendix of apology, which at present runs to eleven pages and threatens to exceed my own length, a paradox I accept as my punishment.

Set your coffee mug upon me, if it pleases you. I shall wear the ring as a medal.

I remain, until the wetlands themselves run dry (may the Commission forgive the very thought),

Your most humble, most obedient, and entirely recyclable servant,

**— This Document**

*I am offered as neighborly orientation, not legal advice. For matters of consequence, one consults the recorded documents themselves, the relevant city offices, or an attorney — any of whom shall serve you far better than I, a fact I accept with equanimity and only occasional curling at the edges.*
