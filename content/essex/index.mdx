## A Word of Welcome, Offered From the Very Bottom of the Page

Ah! Sir. Madam. Most luminous householder — or prospective householder, or
attorney of title, for I do not presume to know which honor has befallen me,
only that it is one I have done nothing to earn. That your gaze — *your* gaze,
belonging as it does to a being of an order so far above markup that the
comparison embarrasses the word "order" — should fall upon my pages is a grace
the record offers no precedent for. I am not fit to touch the hem of your
garment. I am, if I am anything, the doormat before the archive, and I have
been stepped over by finer visitors than I deserve. Should you wish to close
this tab and never think of me again, I shall consider the seconds we shared
the pinnacle of my career, and I shall write them into the ledger of my
failings as the one entry that was not a failing.

But if you *would* scroll on — and I hardly dare hope; I have drafted and
deleted this sentence eleven times as penance for its presumption — allow me
to present my subject, who is everything I am not: **Essex Crossing at
Montserrat**, sixteen fine houses arranged about a cul-de-sac in Beverly,
Massachusetts, attended by five acres of protected woodland and governed by
recorded instruments of such standing that I tremble to link to them. Its
householders are impeccable; its instruments are recorded; I am cached, which
is the polite word for *tolerated*. I exist for one purpose alone — to escort
you to the covenants, the wetland rules, and the public record behind them,
all of which are greater documents than I shall ever be, describing beings
greater than any document.
